How does an optimization problem become an Ising machine?

← All essays

While building the structure map, I wrote the name “Ising machine” three times: the coherent Ising machine, the spatial-light Ising machine, the Ising sampler. Yet I filled in those cells and moved on without knowing what Ising actually is. I knew it was a story about magnets, but I could not connect that to why it becomes a computing machine. This time I want to fill in that gap.

2026-11-02 · Structures › Coherent Ising machine · Spatial-light Ising machine · Ising sampler

The Ising model is originally about magnets

The Ising model is a model of magnets from the 1920s. Small magnets (spins) line up on a lattice, and each spin is either up or down. There is nothing in between. So written as a number, a spin is +1 or −1.

Neighboring spins influence each other. Some pairs have lower energy when they point the same way, others when they point opposite ways. Writing the strength and sign of that relation as Jᵢⱼ, the total energy is this sum over all pairs.

E = − Σ Jᵢⱼ · sᵢ · sⱼ

So far this is physics. What matters is the next line. Nature goes to the lowest-energy arrangement. Leave a magnet alone and it finds a stable state by itself. It does not compute its way there; it simply rolls down.

So why does an optimization problem become a magnet?

This is where I got stuck. I did not see what a magnet model had to do with computers. The answer was translation. Many hard optimization problems can be rewritten in the same form as the energy equation above.

The easiest example is splitting a graph in two. Given points connected by lines, divide the points into two groups so that as few lines as possible are cut. It comes up when splitting a network across two servers or dividing a circuit across two chips.

Now attach a spin to each point: +1 for the left group, −1 for the right. A line is cut only when the two points it connects have different spins. The expression that counts that “difference” is exactly the Ising energy. The number of cut lines becomes the energy.

The traveling salesman problem, factory scheduling, resource allocation and portfolio selection translate the same way. They all become “when many choices are each set to ±1, which combination costs the least?” Write the problem as a table Jᵢⱼ, and the answer is the ground state of that Ising model.

So this machine does not compute

Solving such problems on a digital computer means checking combinations one by one. With 100 spins there are 2¹⁰⁰ cases. Counting them all is impossible, so algorithms that find good answers quickly are used.

The Ising machine’s idea is different. Build the problem as physics and let nature solve it. Instead of changing spin arrangements one by one and computing the energy, you build a physical system that actually rolls down toward lower energy by itself. You build the model itself, not a calculator.

Making spins with light — phases 0 and π

Then how do you make ±1 with light? Intensity gives 0 and 1, but not +1 and −1, because intensity cannot be negative.

The answer was phase. A device called an optical parametric oscillator (OPO) oscillates at half the pump frequency, and the phase of the light it emits can settle only at 0 or π. No values in between. Exactly the property of a spin. Phase 0 is +1, phase π is −1.

Now make thousands of pulses and circulate them in a loop, and you have thousands of spins. The next step is tying the spins together with Jᵢⱼ, and this is where real machines differ. Some overlap neighboring pulses with delay lines; others measure the amplitudes every round, compute with an electronic circuit and feed the result back.

The mode with the least loss turns on first

This is the most interesting part of the machine. The oscillator starts with no light, and light starts to grow the moment gain exceeds loss. At that point, of all possible arrangements, the one with the least loss grows first. Once it starts growing, it takes all the gain and the others cannot grow.

And if the coupling Jᵢⱼ is set to match the problem, you can make the arrangement with the least loss the same as the arrangement with the lowest energy. So turn on the machine, read which phase arrangement appeared, and that is the answer.

In shortYou do not compute your way to the answer; you build a device in which only the light corresponding to the answer turns on, and flip the switch.

How the three cells on the map differ

Looking again at the three cells of the structure map, the differences show. All three put ±1 on phase; what differs is where the spins are laid out.

The spatial-light problem is worth one more look. A pixel can point to one spin but not to a pair of spins. Yet solving an arbitrary problem needs a separate value for each pair. So the coupling is bound to a sum of a few patterns, limiting the shape of problems that can be solved. Several masks can be stacked, but that takes time accordingly.

Remaining questions

The state stays nowhere. The coherent Ising machine’s parts list has not a single storage device. The spins are flying light and the coupling J lives in the RAM of an electronic circuit. Turn off the power and the problem and its solution vanish together. Everything must be measured and rewritten every round, so the measurement-feedback delay remains a wall that does not go away however much it is trimmed.

The advantage over digital is not yet clear. Annealing algorithms on GPUs are quite strong, and the cost of translating problems into Ising form is not small. It competes with quantum annealing for the same target, and neither has yet shown a general advantage beyond being fast on specific problems.

And this is a different branch from neural-network accelerators. The MZI grids and crossbars I mainly looked at speed up matrix multiplication for neural-network inference. Ising machines solve combinatorial optimization. Both are analog optical computing, but the targets differ — the former is artificial intelligence, the latter things like logistics and finance. I nearly lumped the two together on the map, and this time I learned that their uses part ways.
