Youngchel Shin 신영철

An electrical engineer preparing for graduate study in optical computing. I study by rebuilding the structures I read about in papers as small models you can move by hand.

What I do

Education
Bachelor of Engineering Honours (Electrical Engineering), major in Computer Engineering, the University of Sydney. My major covered digital and analog circuits and microprocessors; my photonics coursework ran from waveguides, lasers and photodetectors to optical systems such as optical sensing and optical networking. My honours thesis was All-Optical Logic Gates Using Photonic Crystals and Subwavelength Gratings Leveraging the Kerr Effect.
Now
A researcher at a company in applied superconductivity. I have built several kinds of superconducting magnets and superconducting cables, and studied superconductivity along the way. Beyond fabrication, I operate measurement and test equipment, build the equipment we lack, and develop software for measurement analysis and for managing fabrication.
Goal
Graduate research in optical computing — optical accelerators for AI and photonic quantum computing. I am actively looking for a university and a lab. In parallel I am studying optical computing across the board, and this atlas is the record of that study. It is where the computer engineering and the optics I studied meet, and where I can bring my experience of building and measuring hardware by hand.

About this map

I put this together while studying what structures optical computing is built from, which devices those structures are made of, and which layers it passes through from physics up to accelerators. The structure figures can be moved by hand, and numbers are marked as either values reported in the source paper or values I calculated myself.

This map was made while learning, so it may contain mistakes. If you find one, please let me know.
